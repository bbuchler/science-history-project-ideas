Cross-curricular lesson ideas for tutoring sessions / AUs.

This is a quick brainstorming overview of lesson ideas that target high-value CAASPP ELA skills: reading evidence, central ideas, source integration, claim evaluation, short evidence writing, and research-style thinking. The topics also connect naturally to science and social studies standards.

Claim 1: Reading Claim 2: Writing Claim 3: Speaking/Listening Claim 4: Research

Recommendation: social studies picks two favorite ideas, science picks two favorite ideas, and then we build the strongest ones into the same one-hour lesson structure so teachers only have to learn the format once.

Suggested Approach

Suggested Approach

A simple way to move forward without overbuilding the idea too early.

Pick Top Ideas

Social studies can choose two strong topics, and science can choose two strong topics. Then we can compare overlap and decide what is worth building first.

Use One Format

If we build them with the same lesson structure, teachers only have to learn the routine once, even if the topics change.

Keep Standards Visible

The goal is still state-test reading practice: evidence, central idea, source integration, claim evaluation, and short evidence writing.

Anchor Standards For Tutoring Sessions

Anchor Standards For Tutoring Sessions

These are the highest-value reading and writing standards to build around because they overlap across ELA, history/social science, and science/technical reading.

Show / hide anchor standards

RI/RH/RST-1

Cite evidence from text, sources, and technical explanations. This is the anchor skill.

RI/RH/RST-2

Find central ideas and summarize processes, events, or conclusions accurately.

RI/RH/RST-3

Track cause/effect, sequence, explanations, procedures, or interactions among ideas.

RI/RH/RST-7

Integrate text with maps, images, charts, video, data, diagrams, or simulations.

RH/RST-8

Evaluate claims, evidence, data, conclusions, and whether another source confirms or challenges them.

RH/RST-9

Synthesize multiple sources into one coherent understanding of an event, problem, or phenomenon.

Claim 2

End with a short claim-evidence-reasoning response, policy note, exhibit label, or evidence-based paragraph.

CAST Link

Use life, Earth/space, physical science, engineering, data, models, systems, and human impact topics.

Reusable One-Hour Flow

Reusable One-Hour Flow

This keeps every lesson familiar enough for tutoring or small-group intervention.

5 min hookStart with the weird story, image, short clip, or impossible-sounding question. Then preview only the vocabulary students need to unlock the source set.
10 min source 1History/social science source: central idea and evidence check.
10 min source 2Science/technical source: process, model, data, or explanation check.
10 min mediaShort video, slideshow, infographic, map, graph, diagram, or simulation that students can inspect.
15 min activityHands-on or clickable task: build a timeline, sort evidence, annotate a graph, design a solution, compare sources, or complete a gallery walk.
10 min checkMostly self-graded formative check, followed by a short claim-evidence-reasoning response in plain student language.

Webpage Features

Webpage Features

Each topic could become a student-facing webpage plus a teacher-facing guide linked in Canvas.

Teacher-Facing Guide

A clean follow-along page with timing, discussion prompts, standards focus, answer key, and where to pause for small-group talk.

Student-Facing Lesson

A self-paced page where students watch, read, interact, answer checks, and submit a short evidence response.

Embedded Media

Use short videos, infographics, diagrams, maps, timeline graphs, galleries, or simulations to hit RI/RH/RST-7 without needing a worksheet packet.

Hands-On Anchor

Include one simple tactile or clickable activity: design a road layer, sort animal clues, build a cause map, annotate a graph, or run a gallery walk.

Small-Group Use

In tutoring, the teacher leads the hands-on task, discussion, and evidence talk while students use the webpage for sources, media, checks, and responses.

Make-Up Access

If students miss the session, teachers can share the same link afterward so students still get the ELA/state-testing support asynchronously.

Flexible Placement

The same lesson could work as a tutoring session, an add-on lesson, an alternative assignment, or a quick standards-focused support activity.
