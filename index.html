Science + History Literacy Lessons

One-hour small-group lessons for CAASPP/CAST-style reading practice.

Lessons Standards Lesson Flow Setup

Start with strange-but-true stories. Use them to practice state-test reading skills.

These are standalone, teacher-led small-group lessons built around weird, wild, hard-to-believe science/history hooks. Each lesson uses a short history source, a science/technical source, a visual or data source, and quick self-graded checks on one webpage.

Meeting Direction

The planning notes point toward a practical two-session pilot.

Build Two Integrated Sessions

Middle school should be the main audience because it is the largest testing group. Each session should feel like a strange-but-true story, not a test-prep worksheet.

Use One Linear Format

Hook, video or infographic, leveled reading, interactive task, self-graded checks, and a short evidence response. Teachers should be able to use it immediately in small groups.

Coordinate, Then Claim Topics

Overlapping Reading Standards To Target

The strongest overlap is not a topic. It is a repeatable literacy routine across ELA, history/social science, and science/technical reading.

RI/RH/RST-1

Cite evidence from text, sources, and technical explanations. This is the anchor skill.

RI/RH/RST-2

Find central ideas and summarize processes, events, or conclusions accurately.

RI/RH/RST-3

Track cause/effect, sequence, explanations, procedures, or interactions among ideas.

RI/RH/RST-7

Integrate text with maps, images, charts, video, data, diagrams, or simulations.

RH/RST-8

Evaluate claims, evidence, data, conclusions, and whether another source confirms or challenges them.

RH/RST-9

Synthesize multiple sources into one coherent understanding of an event, problem, or phenomenon.

Reusable One-Hour Flow

This keeps every lesson familiar enough for tutoring or small-group intervention.

5 min launchImage, quick question, and vocabulary preview.
10 min source 1History/social science source: central idea and evidence check.
10 min source 2Science/technical source: process, model, data, or explanation check.
10 min source 3Map, graph, diagram, infographic, short video, or simulation.
15 min bridgeTeacher-led discussion plus self-graded questions: evidence, vocabulary, source comparison, data interpretation.
10 min exitShort CER or constructed response: claim, evidence from two sources, reasoning.

How We Could Build It

These could live as simple web lessons linked in Canvas, similar in spirit to your existing course pages.

Teacher-Led Page

One webpage with embedded sources, video/infographic, questions, and a short exit response. Best for small groups.

Self-Graded Checks

Multiple choice, matching, ordering, and source-evidence questions can be self-graded before the written response.

Shared Lesson Bank

Build a small bank by grade band instead of forcing every course to synchronize pacing.

One 11th Grade Model

Use a more complex source set and a CAST-style data/model task to show high school transfer.
