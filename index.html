Anchor Standards For Tutoring Sessions

These are the highest-value reading and writing standards to build around because they overlap across ELA, history/social science, and science/technical reading.

RI/RH/RST-1

Cite evidence from text, sources, and technical explanations. This is the anchor skill.

RI/RH/RST-2

Find central ideas and summarize processes, events, or conclusions accurately.

RI/RH/RST-3

Track cause/effect, sequence, explanations, procedures, or interactions among ideas.

RI/RH/RST-7

Integrate text with maps, images, charts, video, data, diagrams, or simulations.

RH/RST-8

Evaluate claims, evidence, data, conclusions, and whether another source confirms or challenges them.

RH/RST-9

Synthesize multiple sources into one coherent understanding of an event, problem, or phenomenon.

Reusable One-Hour Flow

This keeps every lesson familiar enough for tutoring or small-group intervention.

5 min launchImage, quick question, and vocabulary preview.
10 min source 1History/social science source: central idea and evidence check.
10 min source 2Science/technical source: process, model, data, or explanation check.
10 min mediaShort video, slideshow, infographic, map, graph, diagram, or simulation that students can inspect.
15 min activityHands-on or clickable task: build a timeline, sort evidence, annotate a graph, design a solution, compare sources, or complete a gallery walk.
10 min checkMostly self-graded formative check, followed by a short claim-evidence-reasoning response in plain student language.

Ideas By Grade Level

These are the likely best fits if we want maximum standards overlap and minimum course disruption.

Grade 6

Roman concrete and roads; ancient water systems; mummification and microbes; materials that built empires. Best for ancient civilizations + physical/life/Earth science.

Grade 7

Trade routes and plague; Darwin island mystery; navigation and astronomy; volcanoes and climate disruption. Best for medieval/early modern history + life/Earth science.

Grade 8

Columbian Exchange; earthquake-safe cities; California water; Dust Bowl; energy revolutions; AI and work. Best for US history + CAST-style science reasoning.

Grade 11

Use one advanced model only if needed: satellites, Cold War science, climate data, public decision-making, and corroborating evidence across complex sources.

Include one simple tactile or clickable activity: design a road layer, sort animal clues, build a cause map, annotate a graph, or run a gallery walk.
