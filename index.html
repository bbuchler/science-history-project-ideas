What I would say first

We do not need one giant interdisciplinary project. We can build a small menu of project modules where science explains the mechanism and history explains the human change.

How to organize it

Use course-embedded projects as the default, then build paired lessons where science and history naturally meet at the same grade band.

Why it fits SBA literacy

Every idea asks students to read sources, interpret visuals or data, compare perspectives, and explain a claim with evidence.

Three Ways To Organize This

The meeting does not have to choose only one model, but this gives people a shared vocabulary.

1. Embedded History Module

Default recommendation. A history course includes one science/technology project where it naturally fits. This is easiest to schedule and still supports literacy goals.

2. Paired Course Moment

Two courses touch the same topic at the same grade band. Science teaches the mechanism; history teaches the consequence. Best when pacing already lines up.

3. Cross-Course Project Bank

A small library of “science changed society” modules that different courses can borrow when useful. This works when the topic is strong but not tied to one exact unit.

Project Module Ideas

Course Pairings To Pitch

These are not assignments yet. They are ways to ask, “Does this live in one course, two paired courses, or the shared project bank?”

Grade 6 Ancient Civilizations + Science

Best fit: rivers and irrigation, ancient engineering, calendars/astronomy, agriculture, archaeology, materials, and disease. These can sit inside ancient Egypt, Mesopotamia, India, China, Greece, or Rome units.

Grade 7 Medieval/Early Modern + Science

Best fit: plague and trade routes, Islamic Golden Age science, navigation, mapmaking, printing, Renaissance anatomy, and Scientific Revolution evidence.

Grade 8 US History + Physical/Earth Science

Best fit: industrialization, new technologies, water in the West, earthquakes and city planning. This lines up especially well with US History 8-2 modules on Industry, New Technologies, Immigration/Urbanization, and Progressive Era reform.

World History + Biology/Chemistry

Best fit: disease, food systems, population growth, trade, industrialization, chemistry, and public health. History handles movement and power; science explains mechanisms.

US History HS + Chemistry/Environmental Science

Best fit: fertilizer, atomic age, fossil fuels, satellites, climate, and technology policy. This works for industrialization, Cold War, postwar America, energy policy, and current events.

Government/Economics + Science

Best fit: water policy, AI automation, public health rules, energy tradeoffs, and environmental regulation. Students can evaluate evidence, stakeholders, incentives, and policy consequences.

Project Module Shape

This mirrors your history course organization, but leaves room for science labs, models, and data tasks.

LaunchOne question with a vivid image: How did this scientific idea or technology change ordinary life?
History LensShort source set: textbook excerpt, primary source, map, photo, timeline, or stakeholder account.
Science LensMechanism: diagram, model, simulation, lab demo, data set, or short explainer video.
Bridge TaskStudents connect the mechanism to the consequence: cause/effect chain, comparison, decision matrix, or claim-evidence-reasoning.
ProductMini poster, one-page brief, policy memo, historical exhibit panel, debate prep, infographic, or short recorded explanation.
ReflectionStudents answer: what changed, who benefited, who paid a cost, and what parallel exists today?

Likely Standards Story

Planning-level alignment only. Exact standards should be finalized once the actual course and lesson are chosen.

History/Social Science

Use the content standard from the course topic. Grade 6 can anchor ancient civilizations; grade 7 can anchor medieval and early modern world history; grade 8 and high school can handle industrialization, reform, technology, and policy.

Literacy Priority

Strong recurring fit: RH/RST source evidence, central idea, visual/data integration, comparison, argument evaluation, and WHST explanatory or argument writing.

Science

Use CA NGSS by course: Life Science for disease/ecosystems, Physical Science for energy/chemistry, Earth and Space Science for water/satellites/earthquakes, and Engineering Design for solutions and tradeoffs.

Standards references used for planning checks: CDE NGSS for California, CDE History-Social Science Standards, and CDE Common Core ELA/Literacy resources.
